Marketing teams obsess over ad creative and copy, then send that traffic to a site that takes six seconds to load. That gap is where a huge amount of ad spend quietly disappears.

## The data is consistent

Studies across industries keep landing on the same pattern: as load time increases past two to three seconds, bounce rate climbs sharply and conversion rate drops. This isn't a UX nicety — it's a direct leak in the funnel.

## What actually slows a site down

### Unoptimized images

Large, uncompressed images are the single most common cause of slow load times. Serving modern formats like WebP or AVIF at the right size fixes most of it immediately.

### Render-blocking scripts

Third-party scripts — chat widgets, trackers, ad pixels — often load before the page content does. Deferring or lazy-loading them keeps the page usable while they load in the background.

### No caching strategy

Without proper caching and a CDN, every visitor re-downloads the same assets from scratch. A CDN alone can cut load times dramatically for visitors far from your server.

## How to think about the fix

Treat speed as a funnel metric with an owner, not a one-time audit. Set a target — under 2.5 seconds for Largest Contentful Paint is a good baseline — and check it every time you ship a new page or campaign landing page.
