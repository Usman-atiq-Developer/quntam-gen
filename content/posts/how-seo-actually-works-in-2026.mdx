Search engines don't work the way most "SEO tips" articles describe. Rankings today are shaped by a mix of technical trust signals, content depth, and how well a page actually answers the question behind a search — not by keyword density or link volume alone.

## The three things that actually matter

Every ranking factor Google has confirmed in the last few years rolls up into three buckets.

### 1. Technical trust

If your site is slow, hard to crawl, or breaks on mobile, none of the rest matters. Core Web Vitals, clean HTML structure, and a logical site architecture are the floor, not the ceiling.

### 2. Content that answers the question

Google's systems are built to reward pages that fully resolve a searcher's intent — not pages stuffed with a keyword ten times. Write for the person, structure for the algorithm.

### 3. Earned authority

Backlinks still matter, but the quality bar is much higher than it used to be. One link from a relevant, trusted source outweighs dozens from low-quality directories.

## What to prioritize first

If you're starting from zero, fix technical issues before writing more content. A fast, crawlable site with ten strong pages will consistently outperform a slow site with a hundred thin ones.

## The mistake most businesses make

Most businesses treat SEO as a one-time project instead of a compounding system. The sites that win are the ones that keep publishing, keep earning links, and keep fixing technical debt month over month — not the ones that did one big push and stopped.
